Interview: Media Integrity in Conflict Zones

By Dennis | October 3, 2026 • 15 min read

An exclusive conversation with Dr. Aisha Omar, Associate Professor of Media Studies at the University of Hargeisa, discussing the role of independent journalism in conflict-affected regions and the challenges of maintaining professional integrity in volatile environments.

Interview Highlights:

Dennis: How do you define media integrity in the Horn of Africa context?

Aisha Omar: "Media integrity means telling the truth while protecting those who depend on us for safety. In conflict zones, a story can be true and deadly at the same time. Our responsibility is to weigh both considerations carefully."

Dennis: What are the biggest threats to independent journalism in the region?

Aisha Omar: "The biggest threats aren't always obvious. State capture, economic dependency, and even well-meaning international funding can subtly compromise editorial independence. The most dangerous threat is when journalists begin to believe their own narratives are the only truth."

Dennis: How are younger journalists adapting to digital challenges?

Aisha Omar: "Young journalists are incredibly resourceful. They're using encrypted social media accounts for sourcing, collaborating across borders through encrypted channels, and developing new verification techniques that don't require expensive equipment."

Dennis: What advice would you give to aspiring independent journalists?

Aisha Omar: "Start with listening more than you speak. Learn the histories, understand the communities, and never fall in love with your own story. The most powerful journalism comes from asking 'What do people need to know?' rather than 'What do I want to say?'"
